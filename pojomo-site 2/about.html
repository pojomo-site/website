About Pojomo

A teacher's answer to a problem every English learner in the region knows well.

Why I started Pojomo

After years of teaching in Thailand and living in several other countries, I kept seeing the same problem: everyone studied English for at least twelve years but most of them couldn't speak it. The problem was often talked about in the media and every new Education Minister claimed they would do something to fix it but nothing ever changed. I wondered why this was. I took a deep dive into education research to see if I could find a solution. We've based our language learning program on what I found out.

Students spent twelve years studying English and most still couldn't hold a conversation.

What the research says

Linguist Stephen Krashen draws a line between learning a language and acquiring it. Learning is memorising rules and word lists for a test. Acquiring is what happens when you understand messages in a language, again and again, until you can use it without thinking.

Acquisition needs input you can mostly understand, with a little that stretches you. Krashen calls this Comprehensible Input. It also works best when you're relaxed and interested, not anxious about getting things wrong.

How Pojomo puts it to work

We write stories and develop courses to help students learn English, with a focus on the Natural Approach pioneered by Stephen Krashen. The method we use is to get students to consume a great deal of Comprehensible Input that they actually find interesting, so that they acquire language skills instead of learning them. Besides getting students to read and listen to a wide variety of interesting content, we also provide a structured program with a clear path to English fluency.
